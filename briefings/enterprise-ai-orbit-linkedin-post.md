# LinkedIn executive post: Enterprise AI ORBIT

Enterprise AI is moving from pilot theatre to governed autonomy.

The hard problem is no longer “which model should we use?” It is whether an AI capability can improve a real business or physical process while preserving authority, control, and evidence.

I have been shaping a simple director-and-architect test for that transition: **ORBIT**.

- **Outcome** — what value changes, for whom, and at what unit economics?
- **Rights** — who may recommend, approve, execute, override, or stop?
- **Boundaries** — what data, tools, actions, geography, budget, and blast radius are allowed?
- **Interoperable context** — which systems of record, retrieval sources, workflow state, and simulations form the trusted context?
- **Telemetry and trust** — can we prove what happened, why, what it cost, and what changed afterward?

The model is replaceable. The durable enterprise IP sits in the context graph, authority contracts, integration fabric, control library, simulation-to-execution handoff, and evidence loop.

That is the architect’s mandate: keep intelligence flexible, keep authority explicit, and make every material action explainable and reversible.
